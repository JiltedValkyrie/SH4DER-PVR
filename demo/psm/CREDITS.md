# demo/psm credits

Third-party art the software shadow demo uses. None of it is in this repository: `tools/fetch.py` downloads it, checks the sha256 in `fetch_table.json` and keeps it in `assets/_dl` (ignored by git).

The ground and the poles are made by the demo's own code, and so is the field's plain sky; so are the town's windows, doors, crates, sacks, people, hens, hills and treeline, and the walled square's walls, towers, keep, obelisk, stall posts, counters, crates, barrels, rubble, torches and paving (`demo/psm/tools/bake_square.py`, `demo/psm/psm_ground.h`). The town's sky band is `demo/psm/tools/bake_sky.py`'s cut of the HDR above (tone-mapped, 512 x 256, RGB565), made in the build folder and never committed.

The settings panel (START; `psm_panel_*` in `psm_scene.h`, `psm_send_panel` in `psm_send.h`) takes its layout, navigation and colours from the owner's own Dreamcast menu code (Jilted Valkyrie), reused with her permission and written again here for this demo's row table and 8 x 16 text: the panel down the left, title, tabs switched with L / R, rows with "< VALUE >" at the right and a bar on the chosen one, hairlines, scrollbar, hints, the hold-repeat delay (400 ms; the rate after it stays this demo's own) and the colour values. None of that project's fonts, icons, banners or logos is used: the text is KallistiOS's 8 x 16 minifont (`minifont_draw_str`, KallistiOS licence), drawn into the readout's existing text texture.

The town's and the walled square's kit pieces are cut in the build folder by `third_party/dc-forerunner/tools/lod/qem_decimate.py` (the halo-64 port's decimator, CC0), imported where it is; the cut meshes are build output, never committed.

The technique is from "Perspective Shadow Maps: Care and Feeding", GPU Gems (NVIDIA, 2004), chapter 14. The equations in `include/sh4der_psm.h` were checked against the chapter's printed text (https://download.nvidia.com/developer/GPU_Gems_2/CD/Resources/PSMs_Care_and_Feeding.pdf) and written in this library's own code and row form, not copied: see its header for where it differs.

The QR reading test (`demo/psm/qr`, image `psm-qr-q1`) makes its codes with the **QR Code generator library (C)** by **Project Nayuki**, MIT License ("Copyright (c) Project Nayuki. (MIT License)", https://www.nayuki.io/page/qr-code-generator-library, source https://github.com/nayuki/QR-Code-generator at commit 9a217d443937f60cc56c1458c39a211d7f2d3af5). It is used as a library through its public calls. Its two files (`qrcodegen.c`, `qrcodegen.h`) are not in this repository: `tools/fetch.py` downloads them by `fetch_table.json`, sha256 checked, into `assets/_dl`, and `qr/build.py` compiles them from there. Its copyright and permission notice stay in its files; a disc image built with it holds its compiled code, so anything that passes such an image on keeps this credit with it.
